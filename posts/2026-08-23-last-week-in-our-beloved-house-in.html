Last week in our beloved house in Montrondo, finally got our car back, cancelled our trip to Biarritz, home again, our 43rd wedding anniversary, big family barbecue and other stories of the week.

Madrid, Sunday 23rd August, 2026

Madrid, Sunday 23rd August 2026


Visiting "La Moncloa", the new village landmark. 
Good morning everyone. 

We are home which was not in our plans. From Montrondo we were going to drive to Biarritz, the place we had chosen to celebrate this year's wedding anniversary. I had been looking forward to it so much but had to cancel when the weather forecast changed completely for the worse. Biarritz in the rain is not much fun. I did look for alternatives but didn't find any. Instead, I am devising a plan for a mini road trip at the end of September or maybe October. The idea is to visit Gibraltar, Tarifa and cross over the sea to Tangiers in Morocco. Let's see if it happens.

A week ago today was our last Sunday in Montrondo where we had been for over a month; the longest we have ever stayed. I enjoyed every minute. Honestly it was a lovely slow paced and quiet holiday with good walks, good company, good food and good weather in general. We managed too to escape the heat of the rest of most of Spain. We had such a good time I think we will go for at least as long next year or more. 

That day we went for our walk to Murias in the morning as we knew it would rain in the afternoon. I came home to make boeuf stroganof and made far too much. It is made with fillet steak and is my favourite Russian dish. I also made too much soup for Eladio's dinners and we ended up chucking it out. I should add I hate wasting food. 

It did rain in the afternoon but soon the sun came back and out came a rainbow. 

"I can see a rainbow"
We went out too and found some of Eladio's family picking plums and we joined in. From there we walked to the end of the village to a place known as "La Moncloa". It is named after the Spanish Presidential palace for fun I suppose and was designed as a  place to rest, meet and maybe debate. It is a relatively new village landmark built in 2013 with the help of Javi, a very smart local carpenter. We took some pictures. I chose one for this week's blog post and here is another one of Eladio.
Visiting La Moncloa last Sunday
I was not hungry for dinner and had my usual; a piece of fruit, a yoghurt and a medjoul date. That's all I need while I am on the pen (Monjauro). 

We tried to watch TV but the signal was pretty bad that night. Just before we went to bed, my sister-in-law, very kindly brought us a bag of locally sourced vegetables including a lettuce, runner beans and courgettes. Thank you Dolores.

Monday came and I got on the scales. I was delighted to be down to 60.5 kilos. I weighed 66.2 on 15th May and have lost just under 6 kilos. For you Brits and Americans that is 12.5 lbs, nearly a stone. I have reached my target weight and now have to maintain it. While I am on Monjauro that is well and good. Let's see what happens afterwards. You know what? If I do put on weight  I shall just go on the pen again. No more stupid diets for me. A dietitian friend of Suzy's asked me if I had any side effects. I don't. On the contrary, my blood pressure and cholesterol are at excellent levels and I feel good.  It is wonderful to fit into what were once tight trousers or dresses. The battle of the bulge has been the bane of my existence but not anymore.

As if to celebrate I made a cake.  It wasn't for me but for Eladio's sister, Adela whose birthday it was the next day. I wanted to surprise her so made that most British of cakes, Victoria Sponge. I put jam and whipped cream in the middle and cover the top with proper icing. But that I would do the next day. I used AI for the recipe and both layers turned out divine with no caving in the middle. 

I wasn't celebrating the fact we had no news from Volkswagen about our new T-Roc which had been a whole week in the garage in León. We had to ring every day to find out what was happening and when we would get it back and things were not looking good. I do not recommend Volkswagen Customer Care, especially not in August when everything is practically closed in Spain. 

I ran out of eggs making the cake so walked to the farmer Manolo's house at the end of the village in the hope he had some. He had just one dozen, fine for me. There is a huge difference between eating supermarket and locally produced eggs, the latter being far superior. 

I had more washing of towels and sheets to do and finally the top floor was ready for the next visitors. As I inspected the rooms I took photos to share here for you to see the top floor bedrooms and bathrooms which I still love. We built the house or rather finished it in 2015 and it still looks brand new. We really love it and it is the first and last brand new house that we have inhabited. Here are some of them including the kids' bedroom which was my study before but now has bunk beds.







The top floor bedrooms and bathrooms in our house in Montrondo
Monday's highlight, as always, was my weekly FaceTime video call with Amanda. It's only a few weeks until we set off for Devon where we will be celebrating her 70th birthday. I am really looking forward to that. Apart from seeing my friend, I always love going to Blighty!

It was on Tuesday that I cancelled our trip to Biarritz because of the weather. I had been checking the weather there every day since we arrived in Montrondo. It looked like it would rain heavily for most of our stay from 20th to 27th August and as I said at the beginning, Biarritz is no fun in the rain. I rather regret the decision now as as the week came to a close, the forecast got better. Oh well, Biarritz will have to wait for another time.

I finished making Adela's cake and went to see Lourdes from the village, famed for her baking, to ask for a candle and something to decorate the cake with. She had both a  candle and some coloured sprinklers, just the thing. This was what the cake looked like.

The birthday cake I made for Adela, Eladio's sister, the elder of the two.
It was just before lunch that we finally heard from Volkswagen. The car was ready and mended. They provided a taxi for us to pick it up in León on Wednesday. Finally, after 12 days we would have our car back.

We all gathered at Adela and Primo's house in the afternoon to celebrate her 77th birthday. She did not expect the present from Dolores, Toño, Eladio and I; a bottle of Eua de Rochas, nor did she expect the cake. I was so glad it made her happy. Here she is blowing out Lourdes' candle. It had to be a question mark as we couldn't find two 7s.
Adela blowing out the candle of her birthday cake
We had a grand afternoon and after a thick slice of Victoria sponge cake, I was not hungry for dinner. That evening we went on our last but one walk to Murias. I was glad to see my equestrian friends again but never got the chance to give them the apples I had bought especially for them.

Sadly, Wednesday was our last day in Montrondo. I was up early as usual and was glad to receive a photo from Olivia of her about to go live and report from the news desk. I haven't mentioned it but I watched her every morning.
Olivia ready to go live on TVE one morning this week

The taxi to drive us to León arrived on time. It's about 1.15h to the city but this driver went a long way round and took at least 15 minutes longer. It didn't matter. What did matter was that we were about to get our new car back. So what was wrong with it? It apparently had a software glitch with the brake pads. Thankfully it was a glitch only. It was great to drive it again. While in León, I wanted to see if I could buy a dress for this week's wedding. On Saturday, Alicia, our niece and Goddaughter is getting married to Luismi in León and we shall all be together again. I had worried all summer about what to wear. I had decided on a white embroidered tunic top from Zara to be worn with black trousers, lent to me by Pili. But I knew I would be the ugly duckling again at a wedding. We went to El Corté Inglés but I found nothing. I wanted a dress with sleeves and to go just below the knees, thus hiding my legs and arms. So, of course I found nothing. It was once I was home I came across an outfit I think will do. It's a simple black dress with a transparent long blouse like garment with coloured flowers. I last wore it on my 35th wedding anniversary which we shared with Phil and Kathy when we went to La Finca near Santa Pola. I dug out the photo to show to Pili, my fashion stylist and she approved. This is the outfit.
The outfit I shall probably be wearing for Alicia's wedding next Saturday
It was late by then and we had nothing planned for lunch so bought ready made food at El Corte Inglés to eat in Montrondo. And there was Pili waiting for us with more food and locally picked greengages, bless her. I went with her to pick more in the afternoon and we ended up in the bar having a drink with lots of other women. It was a good end to the day and to our stay.

We went on our last walk with Toño and Dolores. We watched the TV afterwards and I was very happy to hear of a promising vaccine for patients with Melanoma, that terrible skin cancer my brother died of. Also this week we heard that Harry and Meghan will be returning to the UK. Well, good luck to them. I don't really care. 

 That night I went to bed with mixed feelings. I would miss our house in Montrondo and our peaceful life there and didn't really want to go home.

Thursday did come, the day of our return and I was reminded, as always, by a phrase taught to me by my father; "all good things come to an end". How true.

After saying our goodbyes, we set off at about 10.30 with a heavy heart I should add. We only stopped once for a coffee and were home on time for lunch. We were greeted sweetly by Pippa first, always her first, then by Suzy. It was great to see them both. It was much hotter in Madrid but not sweltering. I had to put the air con in our room. What a difference from Montrondo where we slept with the heating on, pyjamas and a duvet.

It took forever to unpack and I spent the rest of the afternoon/evening, lounging in our room and not doing much. I missed our evening walk as it is too hot to go for walks here. But it was good to be home and to sleep in our more usual bed. For dinner we treated ourselves to scrambled eggs I got from Manolo and we all agreed they are superior to anything bought. 

Friday was our 43rd wedding anniversary and we were to celebrate it at home rather than in Biarritz which was a bit sad really. Eladio and I went shopping and of course I had a coffee at Alverán. We talked about our wedding and all these years together. I wonder if we will make it to our 50th wedding anniversary when we will both be rather gaga. So we agreed to celebrate together as often as possible. Thus the possible upcoming road trip to Morocco. Watch this space.

Oli and the kids joined us for lunch and stayed until late in the afternoon. It was good to see them and the kids behaved quite well. We had a late siesta when they went and at about 7ish I had a shower and got changed and made up to go out for our anniversary dinner. I am afraid there is no anniversary picture this year of the two of us  as the light at La Roca restaurant in Pozuelo was not flattering. Eladio took one of me where I look like my mother when she was old. I don't like it but it's what it is.
Photo of me at our anniversary dinner on Friday night. I don't particularly like it. 
I have to say the dinner was very good. I had delicious itsy bitsy things and Eladio went for lubina, a sort of sea bass. We dame home feeling good and not too full. I didn't sleep well that night and was up early as usual on Saturday morning. 

Yesterday Oli was coming with the kids and three of the girls' friends for a barbecue - Elena, Chati and Juli.  We hadn't bought enough burgers so Suzy and I went to get more. We also went for a coffee together at Manacor. She is looking good and I hope she is enjoying her new job. She was promised 10 days training which didn't happen and she says she is doing her best although it is very stressful. I wish her all the best in her new job.

Tana was back on Saturday and without her the lunch would not have been the same. I got everything out and Suzy laid the table. Eladio grilled the burgers and sausages and Tana did the rest. Just before we dug in, we had some photos taken. Here is the best one out of about 10. In the rest Elliot is making silly faces:-)
Yesterday's barbecue
We were exhausted afterwards and retired to our room while the young ones spent the afternoon by the pool. 

Today is Sunday and we have no plans but that is a good thing isn't it?  It's quite fresh at the moment so when I publish this post, maybe we will go for a walk. We certainly need one.

That's it from me for this week. I didn't have much to recount but hoped you enjoyed the read. Cheers till next week,
Masha.
