Pippa on the mend and bouncing back, a quiet and very rainy week, all the addresses I have lived at, Spain's non general strike turned nasty and other stories of the week.

Madrid, Sunday 15th November, 2026

 Madrid, Sunday 15th November, 2026

Coffee yesterday morning in Majadahonda
Good morning all.

It is now the middle of November, probably my least favourite month and we are not going anywhere.  We have to be here for Pippa who I am so glad to tell you is on the mend. It has been a very quiet and extremely rainy week so we haven't even been on our walks. I do miss the good weather and remember our road trip to Morocco with nostalgia. 

I am sitting here wondering how on earth I am going to write this post as I have so little to tell you this week. As usual, I shall start at the beginning with last Sunday.

Last Sunday was the 5th anniversary of the death of my dear father, Charles Courtenay Lloyd and my most faithful blog reader, bless him. He taught me to get on with things come what may and that's what I always try to do. We did actually go for a walk that day, our only walk of the week but without Pippa. We had coffee at home and enjoyed lunch alone  as all our guests had left and Tana was out. I made the most delicious roast rack of lamb with roast potatoes, carrots and mint sauce. Later she returned to get a room ready for a young student, Guillermo who is from Panama. November is a quiet month for guests but even so we are full this weekend and next. 

I had a good siesta that day except that I had the oddest dream. In my dream I was staying at an Airbnb which was our house at 6 Heaton Grove Bradford!  While I was sleeping, Carlos Alcaraz who has finally recovered from his injuries, had just won the Paris Masters against American Ben Shelton. It was good to hear he had made a comeback. He has now won 7 of the 9 Masters' tournaments and has moved up to the number 2 spot just behind Zverev. Sinner is not in the top 10 as he is now injured too.

The news that night that caught my attention the most is of the constant arrival of boats full of immigrants mostly from West Africa to the Canary Islands and more specifically to one of the smaller islands; El Hierro. With a population of only 12.000, in the last 5 weeks, an average of 6 boats a day arrived there carrying 9000 immigrants!  1000 arrived yesterday and they keep coming. Here we have another crisis this rotten government cannot deal with and has probably caused. Pedro Sánchez who has given residency (and votes) to so many immigrants, especially  this year  has caused this pull effect and is much to blame for the invasion of Ceuta too. We learned this week that 100.000 crossed the border at the end of July when the total population of Ceuta is 80.000. Many went back but there are still some 18.000 left. 

Monday was a holiday in Madrid to celebrate the patron saint of the city, "La Almudena". That was the day I learned the name of a big chip and AI company called Nvidia. The founder and CEO, Jensen Huang, a married man with children, was found to have had an affair with a supposed Chinese spy, in a sort of honey trap. More extra matrimonial affairs emerged during the week after which Huang finally resigned. I had no idea important Nvidia was until this week. It is at the centre of AI and a pillar of the economy worth 4 trillion dollars. Thus, the sudden resignation of its owner has shaken the industry and the economy. They make the chips for AI  for the likes of Microsoft, Google, Meta (Facebook) and Chat GPT. However, the US Government strictly forbids the sale of their chips to China to prevent the latter gaining leadership in the AI race. If Huang passed on any of the company information to the Chinese through this affair, this suddenly becomes a national security crisis. But I honestly don't think Trump seems worried at all. 

All sorts of things are happening in the AI world that are not all good. Supermarket freezers are being hacked all over Europe. Why? For the sake of disruption alone? Probably. That is also probably the reason for so many bomb scares at schools and on airlines too. We live in a crazy world. On Friday night all flights were cancelled in the US in a sudden nationwide closing of air space, like after 9/11 - in the end they cancelled all departing traffic and it lasted 4 hours. The reason quoted was a "major telecommunications outage affecting air traffic control systems".  Later I read, a bit like Spain's railways, their system desperately needs updating! Apparently, communication between airplanes and traffic control failed and in order to prevent catastrophic accidents, air traffic was cancelled. Imagine!!
Microsoft this week also had a major outage which was worldwide and affected most of its products, including Outlook, the preferred email system of the corporate world. Thankfully, neither of the outages were cyber attacks but it does leave you wondering. We are so dependent on IT these days, the world stops when it does't work.

I wasn't affected as I didn't open my PC that morning. Instead, I decided I needed some retail therapy. Thus I dragged my husband to Centro Oeste and after my coffee left him with his phone while I roamed the stores. But I didn't find anything I fancied at Zara, Massimo Duti, H&M, Cortefiel or anywhere else. There was nothing for me that day and truth to tell I don't need any clothes. Instead we went into the Carrefour Hypermarket and got some of their divine smoked salmon and more salt free turkey for Pips.

We had lunch with Suzy that day, quite an occasion as we hardly see her these days. We had a good and frank conversation about her life and how it has improved so much. She really has too and I just hope she chooses the right paths now, so important for her future. 

I don't know why but it was on Monday that I realised we have now lived in this house for 20 years - we moved in in June 2006. That got me thinking about all the different addresses I have lived at since I came into this world. I was born in February 1957 at my parent's first home: 291 Milton Road in Cambridge. We moved to Rectory Road in Ruskington (Lincolnshire) in 1960 because my father went to work as a language instructor at RAF Cranwell and to Yorkshire in 1965. He got a job as a senior master of languages at Bradford Grammar School. We first moved into rented accommodation at Rowantree Avenue near Baildon, a bungalow we lived in for a year before my parents bought the massive Victorian house at 6 Heaton Grove. It was near the school but also had lots of bedrooms which my mother rented to Bradford University students to pay the mortgage. I lived there from 1966 until I left for Spain in 1981. But truth to tell, I also lived in Nottingham when I went to University there in 1976. My first year was on campus at Nightingale Hall and my second year was at Church Avenue with my friend Sandra. My third year was in Madrid where I lived with the lovely González family on a street called Gaztambide. In my 4th and last year at Nottingham I lived at Cromwell Road in Beeston with 4 other students, Adele, Susan, Chris and I think the other bloke was Kevin. In 1980 I moved back to Bradford for my final year at 6 Heaton Grove and in England. 
6 Heaton Grove
1980 was my last year at 6 Heaton Grove but my parents continued to live there, my mother until her death in October 1999, and my father until 2005 when he moved to Spain to live with us. We were with him when he walked out of the porch for the last time and I wrote about the house in my blog that week which you can read here. 

That summer I met Eladio and in 1981 I moved to Spain. For the first two years we lived in an area called "Saconia" on Calle Juan Andrés. In 1983 we married and thanks to my parents' help, bought a flat in Madrid, in the same block as Eladio's brother and his wife, Toño and Dolores. We lived at 71 Francisco Silvela for 5 years during which both our daughters, Olivia and Suzy were born and in 1988, fed up of the city, we moved to the outskirts, to Boadilla del Monte, where we bought a proper house with a garden. We lived there for 18 years until we moved to our current house. Those are all the addresses I have lived at for a significant amount of time and I am now wondering how many more years we will live here. I don't want to think about us being decrepit and too old to travel, enjoy life and this house.

I told Amanda about my dream of being at an Airbnb at 6 Heaton Grove, the house of my childhood, during our weekly Facetime call that afternoon. Of note, in that conversation we decided on dates for our "Mucky Duck" reunion at our house in May. We will be 5 couples, all friends from my Bradford teenage days and their spouses: Amanda, Andy, Simon, Gill, Mark, Sarah, Chris and Gael. That is going to be a blast. 

Tuesday was equally quiet. Of note that day I made a delicious winter soup which Olivia inspired me to make. I roasted red peppers, onion and carrots and mashed them all and turned them into a cream of red pepper soup to which I added a dash of sour cream. It was so good, I shall be making more this week.
 Cream of red pepper soup
We had a late morning appointment with the vet specialised in eyes for Pippa. When she became paralysed she wasn't able to close her eyes or even blink which left them very sore. We gave her drops of course but they were in a sorry state. Thankfully, by Tuesday they were a lot better and she never got an infection. 

She really is on the mend and is bouncing back after the scare she gave us. If you read last week's post, you will know she became paralysed 2 weeks ago and was diagnosed with hipothyroid polyneuropathy. Since  she has been on Levothyroxine, she has improved in leaps and bounds, with progress every day. Honestly, I think even the vets were astonished at her quick recovery, although she is still a bit weak. It was on Tuesday that she tackled the staircase at our 3 story house. I was amazed she did it and she now does it every day, although going up is still a little difficult. When we took her to the vet on Friday she even jumped off the sofa. Yesterday she surprised us too when she finally jumped off our bed. And when we took her to rehab they couldn't believe she was walking.  Thus we have skipped more rehab. I don't think she needs it. 

Pippa resting on our bed. I took this photo just before writing today's blog post


Of note on Tuesday evening, the funeral was held for the victims of the tragic train crash in Urda, at the Cathedral in Toledo. The King and Queen went but not the President, Pedro Sánchez. He argued he doesn't go to religious services which is absolutely not true. He has been to quite a few and he may be a non believer, but that is no excuse.  I reckon he was afraid of being booed at. And he would have been. 

Wednesday was 11th November, Armistice Day. Famously remembered as the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month, it commemorates the peace signed in 1918 to end World War 1. Remembrance Day was on the 8th of this month  and is always on the closest Sunday to 11th November. It now commemorates all wars. 

Wednesday was the day of the General Strike in Spain which was really a non event. Most general strikes are against a sitting government. But no, this one was against the right wing opposition in case they win the elections on 29th November. It all started with housing protests which this government in all its 8 years has done nothing about. From housing it turned to general discontent of the far left and led to lots of violence in Barcelona and Madrid and other cities and ransacking of shops and plain hooliganism. Those organising the strike said it was a  success. The media measure the success by how much less electricity is consumed by industry and workers and it was less than 5% than on a normal day. So it was hardly a "general" strike. The picketers, though, were vicious while most people just wanted to work. Things turned nasty in the demonstrations held afterwards with a lot of anti system louts on the rampage. This is not the Spain I know and I don't like it. Hopefully, Sánchez will be ousted and we will have better governance. If he is not, this country will go to the dogs (why dogs, poor creatures?).

We were up very early for Eladio's flu jab in Madrid. He has to go to a private clinic as he was a public servant and they mostly use the private system. I have both private which I pay for and also use the public system. My jab was later in the morning and I was also offered a Covid jab which I accepted. I had sore arms for a few days but nothing else. 

My Recurrent UTI returned nastily  on Wednesday after stopping the antibiotics the day before so I had to start yet another bout which only really help a bit. I am fed up with this problem and I don't know if it will ever go away. In order to sleep that night, apart from my usual sleeping tablets, I took valium. At least I got 6.45h sleep. 

Thursday came and I went with Suzy for her injection. She had to go to work so we didn't go anywhere together but it was nice to accompany her. 

Not much else happened on Thursday so let me move on to Friday. It was Friday 13th and although I am not suspicious I don't really like that date.

I accompanied Elado to his barber, Goyo, and hardly a word was exchanged between the two. While out, we went for my morning coffee to Alverán and then to do a couple of errands. In the afternoon Pippa had her 3rd appointment this week at the Veterinary Hospital in Villanueva de la Cañada. This one was to check her overall health since her illness and also to do a blood test to measure the levels of T4 and TSH  to see if the doseage of medicine is the correct one. Honestly, we were there for hours. I think her vet, Sara, was using Pippa as a study case for a group of 5 or 6 students. Sara is very thorough, a bit too much and she talks so fast and with little clarity, I often can't follow her. I really hope that after this week we won't have to go back too often.

I was in a hurry to come home as Olivia and family were coming for dinner. But I needn't have as they were with Elliot and Juliet at one of his friend's birthday parties having a blast. Tana made lots of tortilla and I made roast pork ribs which was challenging. I never took a photo of that dinner, but here is one of Miguel and Oli she sent to me before they arrived.
A selfie of Miguel and Olivia sent to me on Friday
It was lovely to have them for dinner. The kids weren't hungry after a birthday party but even they liked my pork ribs. 

Yesterday,  Saturday, was the rainiest day of the week. We were pretty much on our own as Tana was off and our student guests at the University. I needed an excuse to get out of the house so took Eladio to Majadhonda. I wanted to go to a jeweler there for my mother's engagement ring as this week I lost one of the turquoise stones. That ring which my parents bought second hand, means so much to me. As Olivia said recently, just seeing it, reminds us of my mother and her grandmother's hands. She always wore it and I do too. So out we went and were on the Gran Via during the worst part of the rain. Coffee was nice though at Manolo Bakes where I had mine with two mini pistachio croissants; divine. I had no photos for this weeks' blog post, so asked Eladio to take one of me there which I have chosen as the only possible feature photo this week. 

We came home to have lunch alone and I made spaghetti carbonara. We later met one of our guests, a nutritionist like our daughter and also called Susana. She is from Asturias. Nice girl. 

Olivia had time out this weekend when she went to one of their friend's, Chati's 40th, on her own; no kids and no husband. They booked a holiday home somewhere near Toledo for the night and I am sure she had a great time despite the rain. Unfortunately, Suzy couldn't join them.

And today is Sunday and it looks like rain again. Suzy will be joining us for lunch, so that is one event to look forward to to day. 

Now just one bit of trivia before I sign off. I read in the Sunday Times today that King Charles' biggest childhood regret was that he and his sister Anne, never went to Butlins. Butlins were holiday camps with entertainment popular in the UK in his and my day. It is one of my regrets too. I also tried to get my parents to take us and I suspect they never did as they thought it too frivelous and probably too working class. Our holidays were of another nature usually by train or bus or in my Aunty Masha's battered old car to what we used to call "The Continent". 

That's it from me this week. Have a great day. Cheers Masha.
